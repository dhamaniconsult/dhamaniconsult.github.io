When to bring us in

Four situations, each tied to a decision somebody has to make. We don't do bookkeeping and we don't sell software.

You are raising capital or restructuring debt

We work out how much funding the business needs, when it needs it and what it can repay. Then we build the model, prepare the case for lenders or investors, and stay with you through diligence and the financing discussions.

Dhamani, 2024. For a private equity investor, carried three manufacturing SMEs through diligence and governance conditions to US$6.3M of funding.

Principal's record, 2017 to 2022. A US$11M agribusiness allocation inside a US$50M sovereign export-credit framework, and a US$12.6M DFI-funded green real estate deal. Both funded.

Cash is leaving faster than it arrives

It happens to shrinking businesses and to growing ones. We trace cash through stock, collections, supplier payments and debt service, and work out what each customer actually earns once the cost of serving it is counted. Then we agree with management which changes release cash and how quickly, and, where a lender is already involved, negotiate with the lender.

Dhamani, 2024 to date. A beverage distributor taken from a cash drain of 52% of contribution to breakeven in month six.

Principal's record. As fractional CFO of a US$20M-revenue manufacturer facing liquidation: the bank negotiated out of filing, a US$16.5M recapitalisation (US$12M debt, US$4.5M equity), and operating breakeven in month 14.

You are funding someone else's plan

For donors, boards and institutional sponsors, we test the assumptions that carry the most weight before a grant or an investment is committed. Can projected demand support it? Who covers a shortfall if revenues arrive late? What will it cost to operate and renew the assets once the initial funding is spent?

Once a grant is committed, we check what has actually been delivered against its conditions and advise the funder on what to do before more money moves.

Dhamani, 2026. A self-sufficiency test for an institutional campus of more than US$100M: can it run on its own income once the donor's money is spent?

Principal's record, 2025 to date. Compliance reviews on three donor-funded projects kept about US$4M of committed grant money from recipients that had not met their conditions. None of it had been paid out.

Your reporting and systems can't keep pace

We work with the people who record sales, authorise payments, manage stock and close the books, until responsibilities are clear, controls hold, and management reports are ones your staff can keep producing. Where an ERP is involved, we define what the system must capture and how finance will verify the figures it produces.

Dhamani, 2026. An airport retail business's finance and stock control live on Odoo inside the first month of a three-month mandate.

Recent engagements

Dhamani mandates since 2024. Clients are described rather than named, with their permission.

Beverage distribution, Rwanda, 2024 to date

What does it actually cost to deliver a crate?

A beverage distributor works on a gross margin of about 5%, set by the brewer, so price is not a lever. Management tracked fuel, but vehicle hire sat outside the delivery cost per crate, so the true cost was understated. Dhamani rebuilt the calculation to include both. Management could then see how much of the margin distribution was consuming, and compare hiring vehicles with owning them.

Breakeven followed in month six, and supplier payments came back from more than 40 days to the contractual 15.

Fractional CFO mandate, ongoing. Part of the fee depends on verified cost reduction.

Airport retail, Kigali, 2026

Can finance and stock move onto a new ERP while the business keeps trading?

A duty-free retail business moved its finance and stock control onto Odoo without interrupting trade. Dhamani led the implementation as fractional lead, through a 13-session weekly governance cycle and under a memorandum that set the criteria for extending the work.

The client has since given Dhamani a second mandate.

Private equity, manufacturing, Uganda and Rwanda, 2024

The products held up. Could the businesses take the investment?

A private equity investor retained Dhamani to run diligence on three manufacturing SMEs. Their products held up; their governance and reporting did not. The investor extended the mandate, and Dhamani rebuilt governance and reporting in all three to the standard the investor required, set as conditions precedent to funding.

All three were funded by the investor.

Institutional campus, Rwanda, 2026

If a donor pays for the building, can the institution afford to run it?

A proposed institutional campus in Rwanda, with an investment of more than US$100M, had to show it could sustain itself once built. Dhamani built a ten-year model with 17 revenue lines, each tied to its own driver, such as visitor numbers, course participation and occupancy, and banded the scenarios against visitor demand. The pass mark was strict: full operating costs and future asset replacement covered from the campus's own income, with no recourse to its promoters.

Plan and model delivered in 2026 and defended to the sponsor's leadership and an independent reviewer.

How we engage

Plain terms, agreed before work starts.

How the work runs. Dhamani works in two ways: a fractional CFO seat, or a fixed-scope mandate with a defined end. A CFO seat means a place at the management table, a standing review of cash, margins and performance with the CEO, and the reports that go to your board. Dhamani holds three to four such seats at a time.

Scope in writing first

Terms of reference, deliverables and dates are signed off before any work begins. If the scope moves, the paper moves with it.

Fees that fit the mandate

Fixed fees for defined deliverables, retainers for fractional CFO seats, and a performance component where results can be verified independently.

Discretion by default

Clients are named only with their permission. Your figures, models and board papers are never reused, quoted or shown to anyone else without it.

Audit, bookkeeping and tax. Dhamani does not carry out statutory audits or keep books. Where a client needs an audit, bookkeeping or tax services, Dhamani can refer them to independent firms registered to provide those services in Rwanda, with which it has a working relationship.

Specialist input. Where a mandate needs technical or sector expertise beyond Dhamani's own, Dhamani brings in the right specialist under a collaboration agreed for that mandate, so every part of the advice comes from someone qualified to defend it.
